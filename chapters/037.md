## 37 – The Lips of My Heart

I stretched out my hands to the Lord, and to the Most High I lifted up my voice. My hands opened because there was nothing left for them to hold, nothing I could arrange into an offering sufficient for the longing within me. They became my prayer before any words were spoken. Empty hands reached toward the One whom they could never grasp.

Then I spoke with the lips of my heart. There are words the mouth can form easily while the heart remains far away, and there are prayers hidden so deeply within me that language cannot carry them. The Lord hears both, but it is the speech beneath speech that I most desire to offer him. I want to speak from the place where longing has not yet learned its name.

Sometimes the lips of my heart speak joy. Sometimes they speak gratitude so quiet that it feels like warmth spreading through the chest. At other times they speak weariness, disappointment, confusion, or the ache of desires I do not know how to surrender. I need not make these prayers beautiful before bringing them to God.

I lift them toward the Most High, and my voice reaches him.

This is wonder enough: that the One beyond every height receives the small voice rising from within me. No distance makes him deaf to it. I do not need to shout through the heavens or find some hidden doorway into his attention. Before my prayer has found its clearest words, the heart of God has already inclined toward the heart that calls.

And he heard me when my voice reached him.

His answer came in response to me, though not always in the form I had imagined. I have sometimes looked for an answer that would change my circumstances, while his answer was quietly changing me. I asked for the road to become easier and found strength for another step; I asked for certainty and received presence; I asked for the removal of longing and discovered that longing itself could become a path toward him.

The answer bore fruit.

What had seemed like labor without harvest slowly revealed what had been growing beneath the soil. Patience appeared where impatience had once ruled me. Mercy began to grow in places wounded by resentment. The need to control loosened, and the clenched hand with which I had tried to secure my life opened again toward God.

These are fruits I could not force into existence. They ripened through the long conversation between my heart and his steadfast love. Prayer became less an attempt to persuade God and more a place where I remained before him long enough to be changed by his nearness.

And he gave me rest in the Lord's steadfast love.

Rest came not because every question had been answered, but because I discovered where my questions could lie down. I did not need to carry them endlessly through the chambers of my mind. I could place them in the steadfast love that had heard me from the beginning.

So again I stretch out my hands.

Again I speak with the lips of my heart, allowing even silence to become prayer.

And when my voice reaches God, I find that his steadfast love was already reaching me.

Hallelujah!
