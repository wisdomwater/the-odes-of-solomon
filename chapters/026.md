## 26 – The Odes of Repose

I poured out praise to the Lord because I am his. Praise did not begin as an obligation placed upon me, but as something rising from belonging. My heart was with him, and therefore my mouth opened. When the heart finds its home, it begins to sing.

His lyre is in my hands. I feel the strings beneath my fingers, yet the music seems older than my touch, as though I have been invited into a song already sounding through creation. I do not create the holiness I praise. I listen for it, and when my hands become still enough, the hidden music passes through them.

So I call to him with all my heart and praise him with all my limbs. My body becomes part of the song: breath entering and leaving, hands lifting, feet touching the earth, the heart keeping its quiet rhythm beneath everything. There is no part of me that must remain outside worship. The whole person can become an instrument.

And the hymn is larger than I am.

From east to west it sounds, from the place where morning opens its eyes to the place where evening gathers the light. From south to north thanksgiving moves across the earth. The hills carry his fullness to their farthest reaches, and every horizon seems to answer another. I add my small voice and discover that I have entered an immeasurable choir.

Who, then, can compose the odes of the Lord?

I cannot contain him in my words. I cannot arrange syllables skillfully enough to capture the Wonder before which worlds themselves seem like brief notes. Even the most beautiful language reaches its limit and becomes silence. What can the mouth say when the One it would describe is the source of the mouth, the breath, and the thought behind the words?

Nor can I teach the Lord how to live. I cannot rescue God with my understanding, defend him with my certainty, or make his mysteries safe by reducing them to explanations. He does not need my words in order to be God. I need his life in order to become myself.

Who is able to interpret the Lord's Wonders? If I could explain them completely, I would have made them smaller than they are. I would have exchanged the living mystery for something I could hold in my hand. Yet the Wonder remains after every explanation, vast and undiminished, while the one who tried to contain it discovers how small the container was.

So I release the need to contain you.

It is enough to know and to rest.

This knowing is not possession. It is the knowing of one who sits beside a river and drinks without needing to understand every mountain spring from which the water came. The river flows because its source is abundant, and I can cup my hands beneath it. I receive what I need, and still the river continues.

Perhaps this is the deepest song: not the song I force from myself, but the song that appears when striving grows quiet. The psalmist remains in repose, yet the music does not cease. Rest itself becomes praise because it trusts the spring.

Let your lyre remain in my hands, O Lord, but teach me not to clutch it. Let me play when the song rises and become silent when silence praises you better. Free me from the need to explain every wonder I encounter.

I will sit beside your inexhaustible river.

I will drink, and listen, and rest while the song keeps flowing.

Hallelujah!
