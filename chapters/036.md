## 36 – Anointed from His Fullness

The Spirit of the Lord rested upon me, and she raised me on high. I did not climb by my own strength, nor did I make myself worthy of the height to which I was carried. The Spirit came beneath me like a great wind and lifted me until I stood upon my feet in the height of the Lord. There I found myself before his fullness and his glory.

I praised him with the composition of my odes, yet even my praise seemed to arise from something deeper than myself. The song passed through me as breath passes through a flute, becoming sound because the Spirit had filled what was hollow. I gave to God what had first come from God. Praise became the place where gift returned to its giver.

And there, before the face of the Lord, the Spirit gave birth to me. I remained bar nasha, a son of humanity, carrying the earth from which I had come, and yet another birth was taking place within that humanity. I was named the enlightened son of God. I did not cease to be human; rather, I began to glimpse what humanity could become when filled with divine life.

I was glorious among the glorious ones and great among the great ones, but there was no room for pride in such greatness. Nothing here had been seized. The closer I came to the glory of the Most High, the more clearly I knew that everything shining within me was received. His greatness did not diminish me; it enlarged me.

For like the greatness of the Most High, so the Spirit made me. According to his renewing, he renewed me. I had thought holiness meant becoming smaller before God, disappearing beneath the weight of his majesty, but his fullness did not consume me. It awakened me, drawing forth a life I could never have manufactured for myself.

He anointed me from his fullness, and I became one of those near to him. The anointing flowed over every boundary I had placed around myself—the limits of fear, shame, and the small identities by which I had measured my life. Nearness to God became transformation. What touched his fullness could not remain empty.

Then my mouth opened like a cloud of dew. Words no longer needed to be forced from me; they gathered gently and fell upon the earth as moisture upon thirsty ground. My heart gushed out a flood of righteousness, not righteousness held as private virtue, but righteousness overflowing toward the world—mercy seeking the wounded, justice seeking the oppressed, reconciliation seeking what had been divided.

This was the fruit of being near him.

A peace offering was mine, and I was established in the Spirit. The greatness given to me was not power over others but the capacity to become a bearer of peace. To stand among the great ones was to bend toward what was broken, carrying into it something of the fullness from which I had been anointed.

Rest upon me again, Spirit of the Lord. Give birth to me before the face of God until I become fully alive within the humanity you have given me. Open my mouth like a cloud heavy with dew, and let whatever you pour into my heart become life for the thirsty earth.

Anoint me from your fullness until nothing in me seeks greatness except the greatness of being near you.

Hallelujah!
