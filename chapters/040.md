## 40 – The Gushing Spring

As honey drips from the honeycomb and milk flows from the woman who loves her children, so is my hope in you, my God. It is not a hope I must force from an unwilling heart. It gathers quietly and then begins to flow, sweet as honey formed in hidden chambers, nourishing as milk given from the body of love. Hope comes because you have placed life within me that desires to give itself.

I have tried to manufacture hope by arranging favorable possibilities before my mind. I have told myself what might happen, what could improve, what I might still accomplish if circumstances bend toward me. But the hope I find in you is deeper than optimism. It remains when I cannot see what comes next because its source is not the future I imagine, but the God who is already here.

As a spring gushes out its waters, so my heart gushes out praises of the Lord. The spring does not strain to become a spring. Water hidden beneath the earth finds an opening and rises, spilling over stones and running wherever the ground receives it. So your life moves within me until praise becomes less something I offer and more something I allow.

My lips bring forth a song of praise to you. My tongue becomes sweet with your songs, and even my limbs are nourished by your odes. Prayer enters the body; praise is no longer confined to thought. My face rejoices, my spirit exults in your love, and my soul shines in you.

There are moments when I feel this brightness plainly, when gratitude rises without being summoned and the world seems transparent to your presence. There are other days when the spring seems buried beneath stone. Yet the hidden water has not ceased flowing merely because I cannot hear it. Your life remains deeper than my changing awareness of it.

So I listen beneath myself.

There, beneath anxiety and striving, beneath the endless movement of thought, I hear the water. Your love continues its quiet work, feeding roots I cannot see. Hope is being formed in darkness like honey in the comb, and praise waits for some small opening through which it may emerge.

Reverence learns to trust you there. Redemption becomes firm, not because I hold tightly to it, but because I discover myself held within it. What you give does not merely repair the life that passes away; you open within me an inheritance of deathless life. I drink from something death cannot exhaust.

Those who receive this life become imperishable because they participate in the One who does not perish. The spring flows from eternity into the fragile vessel of my present life. Even now I taste what cannot be taken away.

Let me receive it without fear, my God. Let hope drip like honey into every bitter place, and let your tenderness nourish whatever within me is still small and learning to live. Remove the stones beneath which praise has become trapped.

Then let my heart become an open spring.

May your life rise through me until my lips cannot help but sing, and my soul shines quietly in you.

Hallelujah!
