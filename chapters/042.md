## 42 – Death Vomited Me Up

I stretched out my hands and drew near to my Lord. My outstretched arms became his sign, my body opened upon the plain wood suspended over the way. I held nothing back and grasped nothing for myself. Love stretched me outward until my whole life became an offering.

I became worthless even to those who had known me. They believed they had diminished me, driven me away, and finally removed me from their world. I disappeared beyond the reach of those who wished to keep me in their clutches. Yet what they mistook for disappearance was a descent into a place they could not follow.

I remain with those who love me. Those who proclaim me discover that I am alive, and I stand among them, speaking through their mouths. I place upon them the yoke of my love—not a burden that bends them beneath domination, but the embrace of the bridegroom around the bride. My love rests over them like the marriage tent spread above two lives becoming one.

I was not rejected, though they thought me rejected. I did not perish, though they supposed that death had finished its work. Beneath the silence of the grave, something was happening that no living eye could see. I had entered the house of Death carrying a life Death had never tasted.

Sheol saw me and was in misery.

Death swallowed me and discovered bitterness. I was vinegar upon his tongue, a presence his depths could not digest. I descended farther, into the lowest chambers where generations had vanished into shadow, and Death weakened from head to foot because he could not endure the Life standing within him.

And Death vomited me up.

But I did not come out alone.

I gathered a congregation of the living among Death's dead. In the place of silence I spoke with living lips, and my utterance awakened those who had forgotten the sound of hope. The dead ran toward me as though the first morning of creation had suddenly broken into Sheol.

Son of God, have pity on us! they cried. Treat us according to your kindness. Bring us from the bonds of darkness and open the gate, for we see that our death has not touched you. Let us be saved with you, because you are our Savior.

I heard them.

Their voices were not lost beneath the earth. I set their faithfulness upon my heart and my name upon their heads. Those whom Death had numbered among his possessions I named free children. Those whom darkness claimed as its own, I claimed as mine.

The gates open.

The sign of my outstretched arms has reached even here, and the wood upon which I was lifted has become a bridge over the abyss. Love has descended to the lowest depth and found souls still worthy of being called home. There is nowhere left for Death to hide his captives from me.

Come out, then, children of freedom. Leave behind the bonds that named you dead. Walk beneath the marriage tent of my love and bear my name upon your heads.

For I entered Death's deepest chamber and filled it with living speech.

Death swallowed me.

And Death could not keep me.

Hallelujah!
