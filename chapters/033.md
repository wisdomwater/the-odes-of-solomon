## 33 – The Voice on the Heights

Steadfast Love ran toward the place of destruction. She did not remain safely above it, calling from a distance to those who had fallen beneath its shadow. She descended into it, carrying within herself a life that Destruction could neither understand nor overcome. Where Abbadon had opened its mouth to consume, Love entered and began to empty it of its power.

I had imagined love as gentle because it was weak, but now I see another gentleness—the gentleness of something so strong that it need not fear descending into darkness. Steadfast Love does not become destruction in order to defeat Destruction. She does not borrow hatred from the enemy or violence from the abyss. She enters carrying only herself, and this is enough.

Then a voice sounds from a lofty peak, stretching from one end of the earth to the other. It passes over mountains and cities, over hidden rooms and forgotten places, reaching wherever human beings have wandered. No one is beyond its horizon. The voice calls not to condemn those who are lost, but to draw them home.

And before me stands a mature young woman, radiant with wisdom, proclaiming the steadfast love of God. She calls to the sons and daughters of humanity: Turn around. Forsake the ways of this destruction and come near to me. There is authority in her voice, but no contempt.

I begin to recognize the ways of destruction within myself. They are not always dramatic paths leading suddenly toward an abyss; sometimes they are small roads walked repeatedly—the resentment I nourish, the fear I obey, the judgment by which I diminish another, the shame by which I diminish myself. Destruction becomes a way before it becomes a destination.

But Wisdom stands beside the road and calls.

Come near to me, she says, and I will enter into you. She does not merely offer directions from afar. She enters the heart and brings forth those who have become trapped within Abbadon, awakening within them the wisdom to recognize the ways of truth. Redemption becomes something living within me, a new way of seeing and therefore a new way of walking.

Do not perish. Do not be destroyed. Listen to me and be redeemed.

These words reach something in me that had grown accustomed to believing that whatever had been lost must remain lost. But Steadfast Love has descended farther than I have fallen. Even there she proclaims that destruction does not possess the final word, and that what has wandered can still turn toward life.

Put me on, she says. Wear me as one wears a garment until my ways become your ways. Let mercy cover your anger, truth cover your illusions, humility cover your hunger for power, and steadfast love cover the wounds from which you learned to wound others. Those who put me on will not be rejected; they will gain imperishability in the new age.

Teach me your halakhah, then—the path by which Love walks through the world. Make your ways known as I study you, not merely with my mind but with my life. Let every step become a turning toward what cannot perish.

And when I hear the voice calling from the heights, let me answer by leaving the road of destruction.

For Steadfast Love has come down even here, and she knows the way home.

Hallelujah!
