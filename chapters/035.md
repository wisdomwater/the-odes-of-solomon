## 35 – The Cloud of Peace

The fine rain of the Lord overshadowed me in serenity. It did not fall violently, nor did it demand that I become something before receiving it. It came softly, like rain settling upon dry ground, touching every exposed place until the earth itself seemed to breathe. Above my head he set a cloud of peace, and beneath its shadow I discovered that I could rest.

The cloud remained with me continually. I had known shelters that lasted only while circumstances were favorable, and foundations that seemed strong until the ground beneath them moved. But this peace did not depend upon the stillness of the world. It became salvation for me precisely because it remained when everything else began to shake.

All things were shaken and moved with fear. Smoke rose from them, and judgment passed through what I had assumed would endure. The structures upon which people leaned trembled; certainties became uncertain, and what had seemed permanent revealed how easily it could pass away. Yet beneath the cloud of peace, I found myself strangely calm.

I was calm in the decree of the Lord. His word beneath me was more than a shelter, because a shelter only covers me while I remain beneath it; it was more than a foundation, because even foundations belong to the earth that can be shaken. His decree entered me and held me from within. I did not merely stand upon the peace of God; somehow the peace of God stood within me.

Then I knew myself as a child being carried by its mother. I did not have to understand the road beneath her feet or know where she was taking me. I felt only the strength of the arms around me, the warmth against which I rested, the steady movement of one who knew the way. What I could not carry, I no longer needed to carry alone.

And the decree gave me milk, the dew of the Lord. I drank what I had not produced and was nourished by what descended as gift. Grace came quietly, drop by drop, feeding places within me that striving had left hungry. I grew not because I had mastered the way of life, but because life itself was being given to me.

I grew through his gift and found rest in his fullness. The emptiness that had driven me toward so many lesser things began to loosen its hold. Fullness was not the possession of everything I desired, but the discovery that beneath the cloud I already possessed what my deepest desire had been seeking: presence, belonging, and the nearness of God.

Then my hands opened.

I spread them out in the ascent of my soul, no longer grasping for the things that had promised to keep me safe. I raised myself toward the Most High, and yet even this ascent was gift. I was conveyed into his Presence as surely as the child is carried in its mother's arms.

Let the world shake if it must. Let smoke rise from everything that cannot remain. Keep above me your cloud of peace, O Lord, and let your fine rain fall until every frightened place within me becomes quiet.

Carry me until striving becomes surrender, and surrender becomes ascent.

Hallelujah!
