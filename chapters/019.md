## 19 – The Cup of Sweetness

A cup of milk was offered to me, and I drank it. I had come thirsty without understanding the depth of my thirst, and what touched my lips carried the sweetness of the Lord. It was nourishment and delight together, something my soul recognized before my mind could name it. I drank, and the life of God entered me as simply as milk enters a child.

The Son is the cup. He holds what cannot be contained and gives what cannot be exhausted, bringing the hidden abundance of the Father near enough for human lips to receive. I do not climb into heaven to seize the life of God; heaven bends toward me and offers itself. The infinite comes within reach, held before me in the Son.

The Father is full, overflowing with life that cannot remain barren. The Holy Spirit draws forth this hidden abundance, not by force but by intimacy, because she knows the depths of God and moves freely within them. She opens her womb and mingles what flows from the Father, preparing divine life to be given to the age. Nothing within God is hoarded, and nothing given by God is released in vain.

So the mystery travels downward: from the Father through the Spirit, in the Son, into the world. Those who receive it discover themselves at the right hand of God, not because they have taken hold of heaven, but because heaven has taken hold of them. I begin to understand that salvation is first something received. Before I can follow, serve, understand, or become, I must open my hands and drink.

Then the mystery becomes flesh.

A young woman's womb receives what the world does not yet recognize. Mary becomes a mother through abundant mercies, carrying within the hidden chamber of her body the One through whom all things were made. The life that cannot be measured becomes small enough to grow beneath a human heart. Majesty accepts enclosure; eternity enters months and days; the Word waits silently to be born.

There is tenderness in this that overwhelms me. God does not enter creation as a conqueror breaking down its doors, but as an infant received within it. He entrusts himself to flesh, to a mother's arms, to milk and sleep and breath. The One who sustains her life allows himself to be sustained by her, and heaven and earth meet in this exchange of giving and receiving.

She brought forth because of will, and she brought forth because of declaration. The divine desire for communion ripened within her until what had been spoken became visible. What the Spirit had overshadowed, what mercy had conceived, now possessed a face. The gift offered secretly to the age could be held.

And what happened in her teaches my heart how God comes to me. There is a receiving that becomes conception, and a conception that becomes birth. The life of Christ is not given merely to pass across my thoughts like a beautiful idea; it seeks a dwelling place within me. What I receive must take flesh in mercy, kindness, courage, forgiveness, and love.

Perhaps this is why the cup is offered before anything is asked of me. I cannot give what I have never received. I cannot manufacture the sweetness of God from my own striving, nor produce divine life through the force of my will. I drink first, and what I drink begins quietly to become part of me.

Give me the cup again, O Lord. Let the Spirit draw from the inexhaustible fullness of the Father and place the Son once more before my thirst. Let me receive without fear what you give without envy, until your kindness takes flesh in me.

May what enters me as sweetness emerge from me as love.

Hallelujah!
