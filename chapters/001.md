## 1 – The Living Wreath

The Lord is upon my head like a wreath, and I will not be moved. I did not weave this wreath for myself. I did not gather its branches through accomplishment or earn its leaves through righteousness. It was placed upon me as a gift, resting lightly where I had once carried so many heavier crowns.

For I have made crowns of many things. I have crowned myself with the approval of others and trembled when their favor changed. I have crowned myself with achievement and discovered that every victory soon demanded another. I have crowned myself even with goodness, secretly measuring my worth by whether I had prayed enough, loved enough, believed correctly enough, or become the person I thought God desired me to be.

But these are dry wreaths.

Their leaves are beautiful for a season, and then they crumble beneath my fingers. No identity built from achievement can remain secure, because achievement can be lost. No identity built from reputation can give rest, because another person's opinion can change before nightfall. Even the image I have constructed of myself must eventually fall away, because I cannot forever maintain the person I wish the world to see.

But You live upon my head.

Your wreath does not dry. Its branches remain green because their roots are somewhere deeper than I can see. They drink from a hidden spring, and the life flowing through them does not depend upon my ability to sustain it. I need only receive what has already been placed upon me.

Braided for me is the wreath of truth, and its branches begin to bloom within me.

At first I thought truth was something I possessed. I gathered beliefs as though collecting stones, arranging them carefully until I had built a wall strong enough to protect me from uncertainty. I wanted answers that could not be questioned and conclusions that could not be shaken. I thought that if I understood enough about God, I would finally be secure.

But truth is not a stone.

Truth is a branch.

It is alive. It grows. And when it truly comes from You, it does not merely occupy the mind; it sends its life downward into the heart. Leaves appear where there had been barrenness. Buds form on branches I had assumed were dead. Slowly the truth I once tried to possess begins possessing me.

I discover that I cannot know love without becoming more loving. I cannot receive mercy while refusing mercy to another. I cannot gaze upon Your goodness indefinitely without something of that goodness beginning to take shape within me. The truth becomes flesh again—not only in doctrines spoken with the mouth, but in patience, kindness, forgiveness, courage, and the quiet willingness to remain open when fear tells me to close.

This is not like a dry wreath that blooms not.

There is religion that can be worn without ever becoming alive. Sacred words can rest upon the lips while resentment remains untouched beneath them. Beliefs can become badges of belonging, separating those who are inside from those who remain outside. We can spend years defending truth while never allowing truth to grow a single living branch within us.

But Your truth blooms.

It cannot remain merely an ornament upon the head. Given time, it moves through the whole person. What I believe becomes what I love, and what I love becomes how I move through the world. The wreath and the one who wears it begin to share the same life.

And then comes fruit.

Your fruits are full and ripe, and they are full of Your healing.

That is how I recognize what has truly grown from You. Your fruit does not leave the world more wounded than it found it. It does not need another person to become smaller so that I may become greater. It does not feed upon contempt, humiliation, fear, or domination. Wherever Your branches grow, something broken begins quietly moving toward wholeness.

Let that fruit ripen in me.

Where anger has hardened, grow compassion. Where fear has tightened its grip, grow trust. Where shame has taught me to hide, grow the courage to stand beneath Your light without pretending. Where I have divided the world into those worthy of love and those unworthy of it, let Your branches stretch farther than the boundaries I have drawn.

For the fruit is Yours.

The healing is Yours.

The life is Yours.

I need not manufacture it. A branch does not strain until fruit appears. It remains connected to the life flowing through it, receiving what comes from beyond itself until one day its branches bend beneath an abundance it could never have produced alone.

So let me remain beneath the living wreath.

Let Your truth rest upon my mind until it descends into my heart. Let it bloom within me until belief becomes love, and let love ripen until it becomes healing. Let every dry crown I have made for myself fall away, one brittle leaf at a time.

And when there is nothing left upon my head but You, let me discover that I have not been diminished.

I have become alive.
