## 30 – The Living Fountain

I come to the living fountain of the Lord because it has been opened for me. I do not strike the rock or dig into the earth searching for hidden water. The fountain is already flowing, clear and bright, before my thirst has learned how to ask. I need only come near enough to drink.

And I am thirsty.

There are thirsts I have tried to satisfy without knowing their true name. I have drunk from approval and achievement, from possession and certainty, from the hope that another person's admiration might quiet something restless within me. For a moment these waters cool my mouth, but soon I am thirsty again. The deeper thirst remains, waiting beneath all the smaller desires.

So I hear the invitation: Come, all you who are thirsty, and take a drink.

I kneel beside the fountain and lower my hands into the water. It is beautiful and bright, and when I drink, something within me grows quiet. I had expected God to demand that I prove my thirst worthy of satisfaction, but instead he gives me rest beside the water. I drink because I am thirsty, and thirst itself becomes enough reason to come.

The water is sweeter than honey. Even the honeycomb of bees, golden and heavy with sweetness, cannot be compared with it. There are pleasures in the world that are truly sweet, and I need not despise them to love the sweetness of God. Yet beneath every sweetness I recognize an older sweetness, the goodness from which all delight receives its light.

For this water flows from the lips of the Lord and from his heart. His speech and his love become one stream. What God says arises from what God is, and what pours from his heart carries life into whatever receives it. I drink his word until it becomes more than something I have heard.

The fountain came boundless and unseen. It flowed secretly through the world while many passed beside it without knowing what was near. Then it was set in the middle, where thirsty people could gather around it. What had been hidden in the heart of God was poured openly into our midst.

I begin to see the Son standing there like a fountain in the center of humanity. The life of God flows through him without measure, offered without envy to whoever will come. No one diminishes the water by drinking deeply. A thousand thirsty souls may kneel beside the same stream, and still it rises from its inexhaustible spring.

There is enough.

So I stop rationing grace. I stop fearing that another person's blessing might leave less for me, or that my own failures might somehow exhaust the patience of God. Boundless water does not calculate whether the thirsty deserve another mouthful. It flows because flowing is its nature.

Let me remain here for a while, O Lord. There will be roads to walk and work to do, but you have first invited me to rest beside your fountain. Teach me the holiness of receiving.

And when I rise, let something of your water rise within me. Let my words carry refreshment rather than bitterness, and let those who meet me find no closed well.

For now, I cup my hands beneath the living fountain.

I drink.

And the sweetness of the Lord remains upon my lips like honey.

Hallelujah!
