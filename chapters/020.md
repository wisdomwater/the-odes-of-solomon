## 20 – The Sacrifice of the Heart

I am a priest of the Lord, and to him I offer my service. Yet I bring no animal to the altar, nor do I approach with something taken from outside myself. I offer the sacrifice of his thought, allowing the mind of God to enter my own until what I desire begins to resemble what he desires. The altar is within me, and I myself am brought near.

For his thought is not like the thought of the age. The world teaches me to guard what is mine, to measure myself against another, to grasp for whatever promises safety or honor. The flesh fears scarcity and whispers that another person's gain must somehow diminish me. But the thought of God moves differently, because nothing in him is threatened by another's flourishing.

The sacrifice of the Lord is justice, purity of heart, and purity of lips. Therefore I bring my mind without blemish, not pretending that it is already pure, but opening every hidden chamber to his light. I bring the judgments I have cherished, the resentments I have rehearsed, the envies I have concealed beneath respectable words. Let the fire of God receive them.

And let not my feelings oppress another's feelings, nor my soul oppress another's soul. This too is worship. I cannot praise the God who gives freedom while making myself a master over another person's heart. I cannot seek communion with the One who sees every soul and then refuse my neighbor the dignity of being fully alive before him.

Do not kill your own soul by owning a foreigner. The warning enters me like a blade, because every chain I fasten around another finds its other end within my own heart. When I reduce another person to something I can possess, use, deceive, or control, something in me also becomes smaller. Domination wounds both the bound and the one who binds.

So let me not defraud my neighbor. Let me not strip away the covering of another's nakedness through greed, humiliation, indifference, or contempt. Where another stands exposed, teach me to cover rather than exploit. Where another is weak, let my strength become shelter.

And cover me also, O Lord.

Cover me with your steadfast love without envy, for I too stand naked before you. I have nothing with which to purchase your tenderness and nothing with which to persuade you to be kind. Yet your steadfast love settles over me like a garment, and beneath it I discover that I no longer need to steal dignity from another in order to possess my own.

Then you lead me into your paradise. I walk among living trees and gather their branches, weaving for myself a wreath from what grows in your garden. Its leaves do not celebrate conquest, wealth, or superiority. They are the green leaves of mercy, justice, truth, and the quiet joy of belonging to you.

I set the wreath upon my head and rejoice. I lean upon your resting place and discover how tired I had become from defending myself, comparing myself, proving myself, possessing what could never make me whole. Here I need not stand above anyone. Your glory goes before me, so I am free simply to follow.

Let your kindness restore what grasping has diminished in me. Let your steadfast love loosen every hand I have clenched, and let truth cause my health to flourish in the glory of your holiness. Make my life an offering in which no one must become smaller so that I may become great.

I will come into your paradise with empty hands.

I will weave my wreath from your living tree, set it gently upon my head, and rest beneath the branches where no soul belongs to another, because all belong in freedom to you.

Glory and honor to your name.

Hallelujah!
