## 2 – Immortal Land

I saw trees beautiful and fruitful, standing in a land untouched by decay. Their branches were heavy with life, their leaves full and open, and self-grown was their wreath. No hand had crowned them, and they had not adorned themselves; their beauty rose naturally from the life within them.

Their timber flourished, and their fruits rejoiced. Nothing strained to become what it was meant to be. The trees simply received life, and because they received it deeply, they became fruitful.

Their roots were from immortal land.

I looked beneath the branches and ripening fruit and understood that their secret was hidden below. Their roots reached quietly into a soil death could not exhaust. What appeared above the earth was only the visible consequence of an invisible communion.

I have often lived another way, trying to force fruit from branches before their season. I have woven wreaths for myself from accomplishment, approval, certainty, and control, hoping they might tell me who I am. But every wreath made from such things eventually dries.

The roots teach me another way. They do not apologize for their hunger or resist their dependence. They descend into darkness because they were made to receive, reaching until they find what has always been waiting for them.

And beneath them flows the river of joy.

It waters the trees and moves around the land of their eternal life, giving without becoming empty. I once imagined joy as something fragile, appearing only when circumstances were kind. But this joy is a river, flowing deeper than circumstance, deeper even than sorrow.

So I lower my roots toward it.

There are places within me hardened by disappointment and dried by striving. Yet thirst is not proof that there is no water. Perhaps thirst is itself a kind of remembrance, something within me recognizing that I was made for the river.

Its waters do not accuse my thirst. They meet it. They enter the hidden roots and rise slowly upward, carrying life into places I thought had withered beyond recovery.

What God gives below eventually appears above. Patience grows where anger hardened the branch, mercy where judgment had become bitter, courage where fear had narrowed the heart. Holiness becomes less like the construction of a flawless self and more like the flowering of communion.

And the river does not water me alone. It moves beneath the whole immortal land, touching root after root. I do not need another tree to wither so that I may flourish, for divine abundance has no need of rivalry.

Let whatever grows in me become shelter for another. If joy ripens, let the weary taste it. If mercy grows, let the wounded find shade beneath its branches.

Blessed are those who see Your waters.

Bring me again to that river. Let my roots descend beneath every wreath I have tried to weave for myself until they touch the hidden current of Your life.

May the water enter the roots.

May it fill the timber.

May it reach the branches.

May the fruit rejoice.

For my roots are in immortal land, and beneath everything, Your waters are still flowing.
