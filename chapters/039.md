## 39 – Footprints upon the Waters

The rivers rage with the power of the Lord. They overturn what seemed secure, entangle the steps of those who trusted their own crossings, and sweep away paths that appeared permanent only moments before. They are sudden as lightning and swifter than thought. Before I can prepare myself, the waters are already rising.

I have known the desire to remain safely on the bank. I would rather wait until the river becomes shallow, until uncertainty has passed and the way ahead can be measured. Yet some rivers do not recede before they must be crossed. Life brings me to their edge and asks whether I will trust what cannot yet be seen beneath the water.

But there is a sign within the rivers: the LORD. His name is written where I expected only danger, and his name becomes the path beneath my feet. The waters that frightened me are not empty of his presence. Even here, where the current is strongest and the depths are hidden, God has already entered.

Put on, therefore, the name of the Most High and know him. I cannot carry his name merely upon my lips while my heart remains clothed in fear. To put on his name is to wrap myself in his faithfulness, to remember whose life surrounds my life, and to walk as one who belongs to him. Then I step into the river.

The Lord has made a bridge over the waters by his speech. Yet the bridge is stranger than anything I imagined, for he himself walked across the river on foot. He did not call from the opposite shore and tell me how to reach him. He entered the waters I feared.

And his footprints remained.

The waves rose on one side and the other, but they could not erase the steps of the Anointed One. His footprints remained suspended above the waters until they became like planks of a firmly fixed bridge. Where he walked, a way appeared. Where his feet rested, those who came after him found somewhere to place their own.

I look at the river differently now. I do not need to discover a path no one has walked before. I need only look for the next footprint.

There are footprints through suffering that do not become bitterness, through accusation that does not become vengeance, through loss that does not surrender to despair. There are footprints of mercy where hatred seemed inevitable, of courage where fear seemed reasonable, of forgiveness where wounds seemed final. This is the trustworthy halakhah of the Anointed One: the way he walked has become the way I may walk.

The river still rages. Faith does not require me to pretend that the current is gentle or that the depths are shallow. It asks only that I trust the footprints more than the waves.

So I put on your name, Most High, and enter the water.

Let your speech become the bridge beneath me. Let my feet find the places where yours have already rested, and when the waves rise on either side, keep my eyes upon the path your presence has made.

For you have crossed before me.

And your footprints remain upon the waters.

Hallelujah!
