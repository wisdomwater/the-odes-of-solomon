## 27 – The Extension of My Hands

I stretched out my hands, honoring my Lord as holy. There were no words large enough for what moved within me, so my body became the prayer. My arms opened, my hands emptied, and I stood before God with nothing concealed in my grasp. I offered him the simple shape of surrender.

To stretch out my hands is to stop clutching. So much of my life is spent gathering things toward myself—certainty, safety, approval, possessions, explanations, even the people I love. My hands close instinctively around whatever seems capable of protecting me from loss. But before the Lord I open them again.

I cannot receive while everything in me is clenched.

So I spread my hands, and their spreading becomes his sign. My body remembers a mystery my mind can only approach slowly. Two arms extend from one body, reaching outward in opposite directions, and suddenly the shape of my prayer resembles the shape upon which Love gave itself to the world. The gesture of praise becomes the sign of the cross.

I do not know where my prayer ends and his begins.

I stretch out my hands toward God, and I find the hands of the Anointed One already stretched out. His arms extend farther than mine, embracing what I cannot reach. He opens himself without defense, refusing to answer violence with violence or hatred with hatred. Even when human hands close around nails, his hands remain open.

This is his sign.

And the outward-stretching course becomes level wood beneath me. What appeared to be an instrument of death becomes a bridge. The cross lies across the chasm between fear and love, estrangement and communion, death and life. I place my feet upon what once seemed only terrible and discover a road.

The wood is level because no one crosses it by climbing above another. I cannot walk this way through superiority, domination, or the careful preservation of my own importance. The road of the outstretched Christ brings me downward into humility and outward toward those I would rather keep at a distance. His arms point toward both horizons.

Perhaps this is why the sign must be made with the whole body. Love cannot remain an idea hidden safely within the mind. It must acquire arms. It must stretch toward the lonely, the wounded, the stranger, even toward the one whose presence makes my heart tighten and my hands begin to close.

To honor you as holy, O Lord, is to allow your openness to become mine.

Stretch my heart as you stretch my hands. When fear tells me to protect myself by closing inward, remind my body of your sign. When resentment curls my fingers into fists, open them. When I cling too tightly to what I cannot keep, teach me again the freedom of empty hands.

And when I come to some chasm I cannot cross—some grief, some division, some fear of death—let me look down and find your level wood beneath my feet.

I stretch out my hands.

Your hands are already there.

And between them, the way lies open.

Hallelujah!
